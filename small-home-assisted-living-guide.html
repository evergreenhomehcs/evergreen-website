How to evaluate a small-home assisted living setting.

The right assisted-living fit is about more than room size or décor. Families should understand staffing, communication, medication support, mobility assistance, meals, activities, safety routines and how the provider responds when needs change.

1. Ask how individualized the daily routine can be

Smaller assisted-living settings can offer a more residential rhythm, but families should still ask how waking, meals, personal care, activities and appointments are individualized through the resident’s service plan.

2. Understand medication and health-related support

Ask what medication services are available, how licensed nursing oversight works, how changes in condition are communicated, and how outside clinics, pharmacies, hospice or other providers are coordinated.

3. Review mobility, transfer and fall-prevention support

Discuss walking, wheelchair use, transfers, toileting, bed mobility and any adaptive equipment the resident may require. A good placement review should compare the resident’s needs with the provider’s service capability.

4. Ask what happens when cognition or behavior changes

Families should understand how staff use cueing, reassurance, redirection, routines and other person-centered approaches when confusion, anxiety, exit-seeking or other behaviors occur.

5. Look at the actual living environment

Tour bedrooms, bathrooms, shared living areas, dining spaces, outdoor areas and accessibility features. Ask where residents spend time during the day and how the home supports privacy, comfort and social engagement.

6. Clarify payment and admission requirements

Evergreen accepts Private Pay, Long-Term Care Insurance, and Medicaid waiver programs including CADI, BI and EW, subject to eligibility, authorization, service capability and placement fit.
