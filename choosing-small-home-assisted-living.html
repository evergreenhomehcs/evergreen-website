How to evaluate a small-home assisted living setting.

The building matters, but the best placement decisions also consider staffing, routines, service capability, communication and how daily life will actually feel.

1. Ask how care needs are assessed

Discuss current ADL/IADL needs, medications, mobility and transfer needs, cognition, behaviors, diet, monitoring, appointments and recent changes in condition. A strong provider should explain what it can and cannot safely support.

2. Look at the scale of the environment

Some residents prefer a large campus with many amenities. Others benefit from a smaller residential setting with familiar rooms, quieter routines and fewer people sharing common spaces.

3. Ask who coordinates health-related services

Clarify how nursing assessment, delegation, medication administration, provider communication and changes in condition are handled.

4. Evaluate mobility and accessibility

Tour entrances, bedrooms, bathrooms and common areas. Ask how transfers, wheelchairs, walkers, fall-prevention routines and other mobility needs are supported.

5. Ask what everyday life looks like

Meals, snacks, hydration, activities, outdoor time, housekeeping, laundry, sleep routines and personal preferences all affect quality of life.

6. Understand communication

Ask how the provider communicates with the resident, authorized family representatives, case managers, clinics, pharmacies and other members of the care team.

7. Review payment before admission

Evergreen accepts Private Pay, Long-Term Care Insurance and Medicaid waiver programs including CADI, BI and EW, subject to eligibility, authorization, service capability and placement fit.
